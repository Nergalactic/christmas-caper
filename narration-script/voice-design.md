# Voice design

Descriptions to paste into ElevenLabs Voice Design, one per character. After creating a voice, generate that character's lines from `recording-sheet.md`.

Tips:
- Generate a few previews and pick the one that sounds funniest played straight. These characters are all completely serious; the comedy comes from the material.
- Voice Design previews read a sample line. Pasting one of the character's own lines from the recording sheet as the sample shows you how the voice handles the real script.
- Hamminess can be pushed further at generation time with lower stability.

## Main cast

### Tiny Hightower
> Deep, gravelly, world-weary male voice, middle-aged American, 1940s hard-boiled private detective narrating a radio noir drama. Smoky baritone, slow and deliberate, dead serious, with dramatic pauses. Rich, resonant, and much bigger than you'd expect. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *The North Pole. Christmas Eve. Snow coming down like it had somewhere better to be.*

### Granny Slush
> An exaggerated, deliberately over-the-top elderly woman's voice, like an amateur actor doing a little old lady. Very old, warbly and quavering, slow, slightly nasal, sweet and fussy, a bit theatrical. American. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *I told him he had a snowball's chance in hell in show business. He never listened.*

### Comet
> Sultry, smoky female voice, 1940s film noir femme fatale. Low, warm, slow and amused, purring delivery, completely unbothered. Mid-Atlantic accent. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Well, hello there, little man. Somebody call a detective?*

## Supporting cast

### Sgt. Snap
> Gruff, fast-talking 1940s New York beat cop, middle-aged man. Nasal and impatient, gets slightly whiny when stressed. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Don't push me, pal. I'm already crumbling under the pressure. Literally. That was my elbow.*

### Lil' Sleet
> Young, nervous man with a slightly high voice, anxious and stammering, eager and a little out of breath. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *H-hi. I'm Lil' Sleet. Big fan of Jimmy's. Is it hot out here, or is it just me?*

### Silverbough
> Ancient, refined, ethereal male voice, calm and melodious, slightly pompous, speaking slowly as if reciting poetry. Gentle British accent. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Greetings, small traveler. I am Silverbough. I have tended this bar for four hundred winters.*

### Cupid
> Smooth, velvety male crooner speaking voice, charming and romantic, a confident lounge singer working the room between songs. 1940s. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *This next one goes out to a very special lady. She knows who she is.*

### Vixen
> Glamorous female lounge singer diva, theatrical and haughty, dramatic, mid-Atlantic accent, 1940s. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Darling, I lost my slot to Jimmy, and tonight I lost it to Cupid.*

### Prancer
> Fast-talking, slick showbiz agent, brash middle-aged man with a Brooklyn accent and salesman energy. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Prancer. Jimmy's manager. Forty percent. Fifty on holidays.*

### Easter Bunny
> Nervous, defensive, slightly high-pitched middle-aged man, jittery and talking a bit too fast, trying very hard to sound casual. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *I have an alibi. Here. This egg. It's hard-boiled.*

### Dancer
> Theatrical, exasperated female choreographer, crisp diction, dramatic sighs, fed up with everyone. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Do you know what it's like teaching a snowman to move? He had no hips.*
