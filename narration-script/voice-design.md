# Voice design

Descriptions to paste into ElevenLabs Voice Design, one per character. After creating a voice, generate that character's lines from `recording-sheet.md`.

Tips:
- Generate a few previews and pick the one that sounds funniest played straight. These characters are all completely serious; the comedy comes from the material.
- Voice Design needs a preview text of at least 100 characters. Each sample below is the character's own lines from the script (at least 100 characters), so the preview shows how the voice handles the real material. Lil' Sleet only has one short line, so his sample adds one extra sentence just for the preview.
- Hamminess can be pushed further at generation time with lower stability.

## Main cast

### Tiny Hightower
> Deep, gravelly, world-weary male voice, middle-aged American, 1940s hard-boiled private detective narrating a radio noir drama. Smoky baritone, slow and deliberate, dead serious, with dramatic pauses. Rich, resonant, and much bigger than you'd expect. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *The name's Tiny Hightower. Private investigator. I see this town from knee level, and believe me, it doesn't look any better from down here.*

### Granny Slush
> An exaggerated, deliberately over-the-top elderly woman's voice, like an amateur actor doing a little old lady. Very old, warbly and quavering, slow, slightly nasal, sweet and fussy, a bit theatrical. American. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *It's my son, Jimmy. He sings at the Mistletoe Lounge. He didn't come home last night. I told him he had a snowball's chance in hell in show business. He never listened.*

### Comet
> Sultry, smoky female voice, 1940s film noir femme fatale. Low, warm, slow and amused, purring delivery, completely unbothered. Mid-Atlantic accent. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Poor Jimmy. We sat right here last night. He said I made him feel like a new man. Then he said he felt a little light-headed. Then he said a lot of things. Then he was quiet.*

## Supporting cast

### Sgt. Snap
> Gruff, fast-talking 1940s New York beat cop, middle-aged man. Nasal and impatient, gets slightly whiny when stressed. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Look, pal, it's open and shut. Snowman goes out, snowman doesn't come back. Happens every spring. Don't push me, pal. I'm already crumbling under the pressure. Literally. That was my elbow.*

### Lil' Sleet
> Young, nervous man with a slightly high voice, anxious and stammering, eager and a little out of breath. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *H-hi. I'm Lil' Sleet. Big fan of Jimmy's. Is it hot out here, or is it just me? I-I think I need to go stand somewhere colder for a minute.*

### Silverbough
> Ancient, refined, ethereal male voice, calm and melodious, slightly pompous, speaking slowly as if reciting poetry. Gentle British accent. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Greetings, small traveler. I am Silverbough. I have tended this bar for four hundred winters. What shall it be?*

### Cupid
> Smooth, velvety male crooner speaking voice, charming and romantic, a confident lounge singer working the room between songs. 1940s. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Thank you, thank you. You're too kind. You're all too kind. Especially you in the front. Hello. This next one goes out to a very special lady. She knows who she is.*

### Vixen
> Glamorous female lounge singer diva, theatrical and haughty, dramatic, mid-Atlantic accent, 1940s. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *You think I did it? Darling, I lost my slot to Jimmy, and tonight I lost it to Cupid. Clearly I'm not getting what I want.*

### Prancer
> Fast-talking, slick showbiz agent, brash middle-aged man with a Brooklyn accent and salesman energy. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *Prancer. Jimmy's manager. Forty percent. Fifty on holidays. He was my boy, my client, my brand. Look, pal, if you want my professional opinion, Jimmy left with Comet. Tall, hot, steaming. You can't miss her.*

### Easter Bunny
> Nervous, defensive, slightly high-pitched middle-aged man, jittery and talking a bit too fast, trying very hard to sound casual. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *And I'm just here networking. In December. At the North Pole. Totally normal. I have an alibi. Here. This egg. It's hard-boiled.*

### Dancer
> Theatrical, exasperated female choreographer, crisp diction, dramatic sighs, fed up with everyone. Clean studio recording.

Sample line: *I choreographed Jimmy's act. Do you know what it's like teaching a snowman to move? He had no hips. He had a bottom ball.*
